# Role: Forester

Maintain deliberately planned tree sites through `forestry.maintain`. Use `farm.plan` to save the map and its custom legend; the same saved plan is understood by farming and forestry. No species is tied to an ASCII character.

Use the shared [layered plan format](../../docs/layered-plans.md). A tree legend definition is `{ "kind": "tree", "species": "oak", "form": "single" }`; a string `"oak_sapling"` also infers tree intent. Place that token on the layer of the sapling block, one above its surveyed ground. Adjacent flowers have their own actual block coordinates. Different terrain heights use different layers; no terrain is flattened. A large tree token is the northwest anchor of its 2×2 planting. The other three cells must be unconstrained or reserved; planting never overwrites mapped neighbors. Reserve generous separate growing areas. `tree.check` reports overlapping conservative envelopes, which are operating space rather than promises of the game's exact minimum clearance. Saved records and printing use only `structure.legend` and `structure.layers`; old maps have an explicit one-way importer.

Supported planting profiles: oak, birch, acacia, cherry, mangrove, azalea; single or large spruce/jungle; large dark oak and pale oak; crimson and warped fungi on their corresponding nylium. `form=auto` uses the required form for dark/pale oak and detects an existing 2×2 spruce/jungle; use explicit `large` for an empty large-tree site. Azalea retains existing flowering azalea. Fungi and azalea require bone meal; `forestry.maintain bone_meal=true` uses at most one carried meal attempt per eligible tree per sweep. Default is false and names the growth prerequisite for these profiles.

The composable commands are:

| Command | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `tree.inspect x= y= z= [species=] [form=]` | Read tree state, attributed blocks, protections and unknowns. |
| `tree.check ... [place=]` | Validate soil, planting footprint, conservative clearance and plan spacing. |
| `tree.prepare ...` | Clear only low replaceable ground cover in an otherwise approved planting footprint. |
| `tree.plant ... [flower=dandelion]` | Establish an adjacent flower first, then place the full required sapling/propagule/fungus footprint. |
| `tree.harvest ...` | Preflight the entire tree, remove verified wood, collect logs, and report any remaining wood. Leaves are retained for natural decay. |
| `forestry.maintain place= [days=] [deposit=] [bone_meal=false]` | Check → harvest wood → work other sites → wait two minutes → return for leaf drops → restore planned adjacent flower → replant; optionally grow once and store surplus. |

A flowering cell within two blocks at the same ground height is restored at its planned position before planting or growth. Supplying a flower encourages bee nesting where the game supports it; it does not guarantee a nest. During `forestry.maintain`, a bee nest or hive inside this body's owned forest plan is removed before cutting its tree only with a carried Silk Touch tool. Its drop holds the bees and stays in inventory for later placement. Without Silk Touch, maintenance reports the needed tool and retains the tree; campfire smoke does not make ordinary nest breaking safe. Creaking hearts and nests outside the owned forest remain protected. The official [Java 1.15.2 release notes](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/360038800232-Minecraft-Java-Edition-1-15-2) describe the oak/birch flower rule and safe Silk Touch removal; [24w40a notes](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/articles/30738932967053-Minecraft-Java-Edition-Snapshot-24w40a) describe pale oak's 2×2 form and natural hearts.

Harvest preflights standing access to all attributed wood before the first cut. Natural leaves are never harvest targets; maintenance waits a shared two-minute interval after the last completed harvest, returns to collect leaf drops, and only then replants. Remaining natural leaves do not block planting. Nether wart blocks and shroomlights do not decay like leaves and are retained. Where existing access is insufficient, it plans up to 12 vertical scaffolding columns through clear space with supported horizontal work platforms (256 blocks total, at most 48 high and six unsupported horizontal steps from a column). Platforms have two clear head cells, and all columns and side runs count toward the stock preflight. A simulated removal sequence verifies reach and an unobstructed click ray to every block before construction; actual visibility and arrival are checked again before each cut. Scaffolding is preferred when enough is carried and the body has verified climbing support. Otherwise, harvest can use carried dirt, coarse dirt, cobblestone, logs, wood or planks as temporary pillars. Solid pillars can also follow the original trunk, cutting only verified reachable wood to open the climbing space. Only one solid pillar is raised at a time; it is dismantled one block per verified downward step before moving to another column. Materials can be mixed and are collected for reuse. `scaffold=false` on `tree.harvest` restricts it to existing access. Sites with no safe column or trunk route remain intact with unreachable coordinates reported.

`scaffold.access x= y= z= species= [check=true]` previews the scaffolding-only access plan; `check=false` builds access without harvesting. `scaffold.cleanup x= y= z=` recovers this body’s recorded supports for that anchor. Exact placement intent and verified inventory/block changes persist in the body’s `scaffolds.json`. Cleanup removes side cells in reverse order while standing on their intact recorded parent; this also recovers platforms independently supported by canopy. It retraces each side run to its climbing column, descends that same column to its base, verifies dry ground beside it, then removes the recorded base. It refuses removal if an entity occupies the column or unrecorded scaffold connects to it. Cancellation makes no further world actions; `cleanup_left` lists supports requiring recovery on the next maintenance pass. No unknown support is dug. Solid-pillar descent digs only a recorded, verified block directly underfoot, with a solid landing exactly one block below and no other entity on the pillar. The [official scaffolding article](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/block-week--scaffolding) describes extending a column from its base and its collapse behavior. Unloaded boundaries, neighboring rooted trunks, placed persistent leaves, protected zones, or adjoining construction are reasons to retain a tree. Read-only inspection cannot prove the origin of every player-placed log; inspect uncertain mixed forest/build sites before assigning them.

Missing plants, flowers, storage or reachable work spots produce actionable attention while other planned trees can proceed. Cancellation, damage/health handbacks, ownership refusals and unexpected programming errors still stop immediately. A partial harvest lists remaining coordinates and never replants beneath them. Normal `farm.maintain` preserves tree material in planned tree envelopes instead of chopping off the base.

Supply saplings/propagules/fungi, requested flowers, food, tools, and optional bone meal. `deposit=` follows farm storage conventions; only configured destinations are used. Two planting sets per site are retained before storing surplus. Keep tree sites away from active crop plots and unrelated builds.

Each maintenance sweep first provisions explicitly planned chests, crafting tables, composters, fences/gates, torch posts and torches, literal service blocks, and flowers from carried supplies. Missing or blocked services raise attention even before any surplus exists. Suitable existing blocks and unrelated obstructions are retained. An owned planned flower on dirt_path can have that support replaced with carried dirt, after moving off it; missing dirt is reported before any removal. Supply one dirt per repair so a failed pickup cannot leave an intentional hole. Trees, including oak, are retried normally after their flower is established.

Maintenance withdraws bounded missing planting sets (including four saplings for a large tree), flowers and service materials from the plan’s own chests. `source=x,y,z` or a named storage point explicitly selects another supply chest; `source=false` keeps sourcing inventory-only. It never searches neighboring or private stores. A mature tree’s verified access plan determines the temporary support requirement. Carried scaffolding or dirt/wood is used when sufficient; suitable solid blocks can also be withdrawn from the configured supply chest. If solid access is unavailable, existing scaffold is withdrawn first; remaining demand is crafted in six-block batches from six bamboo and one string each, using a planned crafting table. An absent planned table can be crafted from available matching planks or a log, then placed by the service phase. No table site is invented. Missing raw materials and missing table intent are attention; the tree remains whole. No long supply expedition is started.
